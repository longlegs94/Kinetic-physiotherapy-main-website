Plantar fasciitis — pain along the bottom of the heel and foot, often worst with the first
steps in the morning — is one of the more stubborn conditions people deal with. Shockwave
therapy is one option that sometimes comes up in treatment plans. Here's what it is and how
it may fit in.

## What is shockwave therapy?

Shockwave therapy uses acoustic pressure waves delivered through a handheld device against
the skin over the affected area. The idea is that the mechanical stimulus may encourage the
body's natural healing response in tissue that hasn't resolved on its own with time and
conservative care.

## What a session typically involves

- A brief assessment to confirm the area and check that shockwave is appropriate for you
- Application of gel and the handheld device over the treatment area
- A series of pulses, which can feel like a tapping or mildly uncomfortable sensation
- A short session overall, often as one part of a broader visit

Your practitioner will assess whether shockwave therapy is a reasonable option for your
specific presentation — it isn't automatically the right fit for everyone with foot pain.

## Why it's often considered for plantar fasciitis

Plantar fasciitis that hasn't responded to rest, stretching, and basic conservative measures
is sometimes described as more persistent or "chronic." In these cases, some practitioners
discuss shockwave therapy as an additional option to consider alongside other care, rather
than as a stand-alone fix.

> Shockwave therapy may help support recovery for some patients, but it isn't a guaranteed
> cure and results vary. Your practitioner will discuss whether it's appropriate and what a
> realistic plan might look like for you.

## How it may fit with other treatment

Shockwave therapy is often just one piece of a broader plan. Depending on your assessment,
your practitioner might also recommend:

- **[Physiotherapy](/physiotherapy-maple-ridge)** for stretching, strengthening, and gait review
- **[Orthotics or bracing](/orthotics-bracing-maple-ridge)** to support the foot day to day
- **[Massage therapy](/massage-therapy-maple-ridge)** for surrounding muscle tension

## What to expect afterward

Some people notice mild soreness after a session, similar to post-exercise discomfort. As
with any treatment, individual responses vary, and your practitioner will monitor how you're
progressing over a series of visits before deciding on next steps.
