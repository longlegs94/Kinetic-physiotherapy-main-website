Pregnancy can bring new aches, tension, and shifts in posture as your body changes. Pregnancy
(prenatal) massage is a common option people ask about, and it's a little different from a
typical massage therapy visit. Here's a general overview of what to expect.

## What pregnancy massage focuses on

Prenatal massage generally aims to ease common tension areas — like the low back, hips, and
shoulders — using techniques adapted for comfort and safety at different stages of pregnancy.
Your registered massage therapist will assess your history and how you're feeling before
tailoring the session.

## Positioning and comfort

As pregnancy progresses, lying flat on your stomach or back isn't always comfortable or
appropriate. Depending on the stage of pregnancy, sessions may use:

- Side-lying positioning with supportive pillows or bolsters
- Semi-reclined positioning for earlier stages
- Adjusted pressure and techniques based on your comments during the session

Always let your therapist know if a position or pressure doesn't feel right — the plan can
be adjusted at any point.

## Before you book

It's worth mentioning a few things to your therapist ahead of time:

- How far along you are and any pregnancy-related complications
- Any restrictions or notes from your doctor or midwife
- Areas of particular discomfort or sensitivity

> Pregnancy massage may help support comfort for some people, but it isn't appropriate for
> every pregnancy or every stage. Always check with your doctor or midwife if you have any
> pregnancy complications or concerns before booking, and let your therapist know about your
> full health history.

## What a session typically involves

Expect an intake conversation before the hands-on portion begins, covering your comfort
level and any areas to focus on or avoid. Sessions are generally similar in length to a
standard massage appointment, adapted for your comfort.

## Pairing massage with other support

Some people also find value in combining massage with other services during pregnancy or
postpartum, such as [physiotherapy](/physiotherapy-maple-ridge) for pelvic or postural
concerns, once cleared by their doctor. Your practitioner will assess what combination of
care makes sense for you.
