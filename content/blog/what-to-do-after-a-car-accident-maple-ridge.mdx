Being in a car accident is stressful, and it's normal to feel shaken, sore, or unsure of
what to do next. This guide walks through practical first steps and how treatment can
support your recovery. It's general information, not medical or legal advice — always
follow the guidance of your doctor and ICBC.

## 1. Prioritize your safety and health first

If you or anyone else is injured, call 911. Even if you feel "okay" right after a crash,
some injuries — like whiplash-type neck strain — can take hours or days to show up. If you
notice new pain, stiffness, headaches, dizziness, or reduced movement, check in with a
healthcare provider.

## 2. Report your claim to ICBC

In British Columbia, you can start an ICBC claim online or by phone. You'll receive a claim
number, which you'll use when arranging treatment. Keep this number handy — clinics will
ask for it.

> confirm the details with ICBC.

## 3. Start recovery early when appropriate

Research generally supports staying gently active after many soft-tissue injuries rather
than resting completely. A practitioner can assess your specific situation and recommend an
appropriate plan. Treatments that may be part of accident recovery include:

- **[Physiotherapy](/physiotherapy-maple-ridge)** for pain, mobility, and guided rehab
- **[Registered massage therapy](/massage-therapy-maple-ridge)** for muscle tension and soreness
- **[Kinesiology / active rehab](/kinesiology-active-rehab-maple-ridge)** to rebuild strength and confidence
- **[Chiropractic](/chiropractor-maple-ridge)** and **[acupuncture](/acupuncture-maple-ridge)** as part of a broader plan

## 4. Keep track of your symptoms and appointments

Note how you're feeling day to day, what makes symptoms better or worse, and any
appointments you attend. This helps your care team adjust your plan and keeps your records
organized.
